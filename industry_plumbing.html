Call workflow

Plumbing overflow answering service

Plumbing overflow begins while your office is open but another call cannot be answered. Revo can capture that additional request using your greeting and approved questions. This is a capacity workflow: it should support the office’s active follow-up process rather than imply a separate emergency dispatch or an automatically booked visit.

Define when an unanswered call reaches the answering workflow and who owns the captured request. Separate a new service inquiry from an existing appointment or an active-job question. The office should receive the caller’s description, contact details and requested next step, with open work assigned rather than left as an unowned message.

Booking and team transfers depend on the selected plan and supported configuration. A requested time is not a confirmed appointment. Apply your business’s actual hours, approved contacts and fallback rules.

Intake does not diagnose a leak, select a repair or promise attendance. Supported booking and team-transfer settings do not authorize the assistant to change an agreed scope or invent crew availability.

Information to collect

  • Caller name and callback number

  • Service address and request category

  • New inquiry or existing appointment/job

  • Issue in the caller’s own words

  • Requested callback or visit preference

  • Assigned office contact and unavailable-contact fallback

How is plumbing overflow different from 24/7 coverage?

Overflow describes a busy open office. Around-the-clock coverage also includes calls outside office hours, which need their own availability policy. Use different rules for those triggers within the same business process.

Plumbing calls: make the handoff usable.

These are proposed script tests, not customer recordings, testimonials or measured results. Use your actual hours, service area and approved contacts.

A new repair request

A caller reports a leaking kitchen fixture. Ask which fixture, the service address and callback number; record the issue in the caller’s own words.

Next step: Route the request using your approved rules. Do not select a repair or promise a crew arrival.

An office-hours overflow call

The office is open but occupied. Capture whether this is a new service inquiry or a question about an active job.

Next step: Assign the captured request to the office follow-up owner. A callback request is not a confirmed visit.

A closed-office appointment change

A customer wants to change a scheduled visit over a holiday. Capture the existing reference, requested timing and callback details.

Next step: Give the next review window your office actually supports. Leave the current booking unchanged until an authorized process confirms the change.

Review before routing customer calls

  • Test callback details, service-area fit and the request category.
  • Confirm which system receives the request and who owns follow-up.
  • Test an unavailable contact and the next step the caller hears.
  • Check the current plan: team transfers and field-service connections are excluded from Solo.
