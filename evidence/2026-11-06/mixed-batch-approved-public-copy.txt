# Painter Answering Service: Book Estimates While You Paint

Painters miss calls for an obvious reason: their hands are full and the job is on a ladder or behind a drop cloth. A painter answering service picks up those calls, collects the details and books the estimate, so a new project does not go to the next name on the list.

Published by Revo, which sells an AI receptionist for home service businesses. Reviewed November 6, 2026.

## Calls a painting company gets
Most calls fall into a few groups:

- **Interior estimates**: rooms, ceilings, cabinets or trim.
- **Exterior estimates**: siding, decks, fences or a full house.
- **Current customers**: color questions, start dates or a touch-up after the job.
- **Commercial or property managers**: units between tenants or common areas.

Estimate requests get booked. Current customers get a message to the project lead.

## What to capture on an estimate request
Ask for the name, callback number and address, interior or exterior, rough size of the job and when they want it done. Ask whether they have colors picked out and whether anyone lives in the home during the work. Good notes let you show up ready to measure and price.

## Set the right expectations
The answering service should not quote a price on the phone. Painting prices depend on surface condition, prep work and paint choice. The safer script books the estimate visit and says who will follow up.

## Sort the calls by job size
Handyman calls cover a wide range. Write down how each type should be handled:

- **Small repairs**: a door that sticks, a loose railing, drywall patches or mounting a TV.
- **Punch lists**: several small jobs in one visit, often before a sale or a move.
- **Larger projects**: bathroom updates or work that needs a licensed trade.
- **Existing customers**: rescheduling or a question about a finished job.

Small repairs and punch lists can usually be booked. Larger projects may need a visit before you give a price, or a referral if the work is outside what you do.

## What to capture on every call
Get the name, callback number, address and a short list of the tasks. Ask whether the caller will provide materials or expects you to bring them. A clear task list lets you plan the visit and bring the right tools.

## Keep the promises realistic
The answering service should not promise a price or confirm work you do not take. Give it a clear list of the jobs you do and the ones you refer out.
