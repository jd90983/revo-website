# Emergency Call Answering Service: Triage Urgent Calls

An emergency call answering service is only useful if it knows what your business treats as an emergency. Without clear rules, every late call gets the same treatment, and the urgent ones wait with the routine ones.

Published by Revo, which sells an AI receptionist for home service businesses. Reviewed November 5, 2026.

## Define what counts as an emergency
Write your list before you choose any service. For most home service businesses, the short list looks like this:

- **No heat or no cooling** in extreme weather, or for a household with vulnerable people.
- **An active leak** or a burst pipe that is damaging the home.
- **No power** to the home or a part of it, or a burning smell from equipment.
- **A lockout** or a security problem the caller cannot wait on.

Everything else, such as a dripping faucet, a tune-up or a quote request, is routine. Routine calls get booked into the next open slot.

## How urgent calls should be handled
Every urgent call should follow the same three steps: capture, triage, act. Capture means the name, address, callback number and what is happening right now. Triage means checking the problem against your list, and act means a transfer to the person on call or a fast callback, depending on your rules.

If the caller is in danger, such as a gas smell or sparking wires, the script should tell them to leave and call emergency services first.

## What not to promise
An emergency answering service should never promise an arrival time it cannot control. The safer script confirms that the request is logged as urgent, says who will call back, and stops there. Your on-call technician sets the arrival time.

# Weekend Answering Service for Home Service Businesses

Homeowners notice problems on weekends, when they are home. A weekend answering service lets you answer those calls without paying someone to sit by the phone all Saturday.

Published by Revo, which sells an AI receptionist for home service businesses. Reviewed November 5, 2026.

## Decide what gets booked and what gets escalated
Most weekend calls are not emergencies. They are quote requests, maintenance questions and jobs that can wait for a weekday slot. Book those straight into your calendar so the caller hangs up with a time.

Keep a short escalation list for the calls that cannot wait, such as active leaks or no heat. Those go to the person on call, and everything else waits for Monday.

## Tell callers what happens on Monday
A weekend caller who hears nothing specific often keeps dialing other companies. The script should say what happens next in plain words: who will call, which day and what they will confirm. If the job is booked, repeat the day and the address before the call ends.

## Plan holiday schedules ahead
A holiday answering service needs the same rules as weekends, plus a calendar. Before each holiday, update three things:

- **Your hours**, so callers hear that the office is closed that day.
- **Your on-call contact**, if it changes for the holiday.
- **Your first open slot**, so bookings land on a day the crew is working.

Changing these a week early avoids booking jobs on a day nobody is working.

## Calls a contractor's phone line gets
Most contractor calls fall into a few groups. Sort them before you choose a service:

- **New job requests**: a homeowner wants a quote or an estimate visit.
- **Current customers**: questions about schedule, materials or the next step on their project.
- **Urgent problems**: a leak, a safety issue or damage on an active job.

Coverage during the workday matters as much as nights and weekends. If your office is small, calls pile up while you are on site. Set clear rules for each time block: who gets transferred, what gets booked and what waits for a callback.
