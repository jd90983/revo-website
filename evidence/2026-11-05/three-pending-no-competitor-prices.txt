# Lower answering service cost: reconcile the bill first

To lower an answering service bill, first work out what the bill represents. A busier month, a billing rule or a one-time charge can explain an increase; an unexplained amount is a question, not proof of an overcharge.

Published by Revo, which sells an AI receptionist for home service businesses. Reviewed November 5, 2026.

## Put the invoice and its rules in the same period
Gather the invoice, the agreement and the activity records you are allowed to inspect. Write down the billing unit, included allowance, reset date, rate after the allowance and rounding method. A blank rate is not zero, and the [service agreement checklist](https://www.revoapp.ai/ai-answering-service/answering-service-contract-checklist) helps you find what is missing.

## Rebuild the bill line by line
Break the invoice into its parts and check each one against the agreement:
- **Monthly base:** matches the plan you signed.
- **Usage:** billable units after rounding, minus the allowance, times the rate after the allowance.
- **Add-ons:** each one approved and still in use.
- **One-time items:** setup fees and credits, kept apart from recurring cost.

Check whether each call rounds up separately. When billed usage has no supporting record, ask which activity it covers and keep the item open until the provider answers.

## Compare options on the same workload
Recurring cost is monthly base + usage + recurring add-ons. Price every option, including your current plan, with the same month of activity and coverage. A credit or an ended setup fee is not a lasting reduction, and a bundled feature is not a second saving.

A larger allowance can cost more at lower volume. Reconcile the next invoice before calling any change a result.

## Frequently asked questions
### Does a difference on the invoice prove an overcharge?
No. Rule out missing records, period, billing unit and rounding first, then ask for the supporting activity.
### Should I cut call handling to shorten billed time?
No. Removing callback checks or needed questions shifts correction work to your office. Fix avoidable handling, not required coverage.
### Does this guide promise savings?
No. It is a method to use with your own records.

# AI receptionist return on investment: a net-value worksheet

An AI receptionist return on investment calculation should answer one question: does the extra value you can fairly attribute to the service exceed its full cost? Answered calls and gross job value are signals, not the return.

Published by Revo, which sells an AI receptionist for home service businesses. Reviewed November 5, 2026.

## Choose the alternative you are comparing
Describe what happens without the purchase, including callbacks your team already makes. A caller who would have booked after a callback is not an extra job created by the service.

## Build the worksheet from five entries
Use one period for every entry and note the source or assumption beside each one.
- **A. Extra job contribution:** additional completed work minus the job costs it adds (parts, labor, travel).
- **B. Avoided expense:** a cash cost that actually disappears.
- **C. Recurring service cost:** subscription + expected usage + required add-ons.
- **D. Retained operating cost:** paid time for review, corrections and handoffs.
- **E. One-time launch cost:** setup and paid implementation work.

## Run the three steps
1. Recurring net benefit = A + B − C − D.
2. First-period net benefit = recurring net benefit − E.
3. First-period return = first-period net benefit ÷ (C + D + E), only when that total is above zero.

If recurring net benefit is zero or negative, there is no payback period under those assumptions. Also run a lower-demand case.

Record owner time freed from the phones separately unless it produces a real cash saving, and never count it twice.

## Frequently asked questions
### Is every answered call additional revenue?
No. Some calls are duplicates, existing-customer questions or inquiries your office would have recovered anyway.
### Is the calculated return guaranteed?
No. It depends on the inputs you supply.
### What if I cannot estimate extra completed jobs?
Leave the benefit unknown and compare affordability and fit instead. Do not invent a conversion lift.
